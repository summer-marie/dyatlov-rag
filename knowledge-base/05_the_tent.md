# The Tent

## Condition When Found

On 26 February the searchers found the group's abandoned and badly damaged tent on Kholat Syakhl. The campsite baffled the search party. Mikhail Sharavin, the student who found the tent, said "the tent was half torn down and covered with snow. It was empty, and all the group's belongings and shoes had been left behind."

## The Cuts

Investigators said the tent had been cut open from inside. The tent had collapsed from the side but not in a horizontal direction.

## Footprints

Nine sets of footprints, left by people wearing only socks, a single shoe or even barefoot, could be followed, leading down to the edge of a nearby wood on the opposite side of the pass, 1.5 km (1 mi) to the north-east. After 500 m (1,600 ft) these tracks were covered with snow. Footprint patterns leading away from the tent were inconsistent with someone, let alone a group of nine people, running in panic from either real or imagined danger. All the footprints leading away from the tent and toward the woods were consistent with individuals walking at a normal pace.

## Items Left Behind

All the group's belongings and shoes had been left behind inside the tent. Although the temperature was low, about −25 to −30 °C (−13 to −22 °F) with a storm blowing, the dead were only partially dressed. Some had only one shoe, while others wore only socks. On top of the tent, a flashlight also was left turned on, maybe intentionally so the hikers could find their way back to the tent once the winds subsided.

## Significance

The inquest files state that the tent had been ripped open from within, and that traces from the camp showed that all group members left the campsite of their own accord, on foot. Journalists reporting on the available parts of the inquest files claim that it states there were no indications of other people nearby on Kholat Syakhl apart from the nine travellers. Photographs of the tent allegedly show it was erected incorrectly, something the experienced hikers were unlikely to have done.
