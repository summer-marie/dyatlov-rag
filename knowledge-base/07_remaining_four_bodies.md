# The Remaining Four Bodies

## Discovery

Finding the remaining four travellers took more than two months. They finally were found on 4 May under four metres (13 ft) of snow in a ravine 75 m (246 ft) further into the woods from the pine tree.

## Location

The four bodies were found in a ravine 75 m (246 ft) further into the woods from the pine tree, buried under four metres (13 ft) of snow. All four bodies were found at the bottom of a creek in a running stream of water. According to the ICRF reconstruction, the remaining four, equipped with warm clothing and footwear, were trying to find or build a better camping place in the forest further down the slope, and their bodies were found 70 m from the fireplace, under several metres of snow with traumas indicating that they had fallen into a snow hole formed above a stream.

## Lyudmila Dubinina

Dubinina had major chest fractures. According to Boris Vozrozhdenny, the force required to cause such damage would have been extremely high, comparable to that of a car crash. Notably, her body had no external wounds associated with the bone fractures, as if she had been subjected to a high level of pressure. Dubinina was missing her tongue, eyes, part of the lips, as well as facial tissue and a fragment of skull bone. She was wearing Krivonishenko's burned, torn trousers, and her left foot and shin were wrapped in a torn jacket. Her cause of death was internal bleeding from severe chest trauma.

## Alexander Zolotaryov

Zolotaryov had major chest fractures. According to Boris Vozrozhdenny, the force required to cause such damage would have been extremely high, comparable to that of a car crash. Notably, his body had no external wounds associated with the bone fractures, as if he had been subjected to a high level of pressure. Zolotaryov had his eyeballs missing. His cause of death was severe chest trauma. Zolotaryov was 38 years old and was studying for his master's certificate in ski instruction and mountain hiking.

## Nicolas Thibeaux-Brignolles

Thibeaux-Brignolles had major skull damage. According to Boris Vozrozhdenny, the force required to cause such damage would have been extremely high, comparable to that of a car crash. Notably, his body had no external wounds associated with the bone fractures, as if he had been subjected to a high level of pressure. His cause of death was a fatal skull injury.

## Alexander Kolevatov

Kolevatov's cause of death was hypothermia. His body had soft tissue damage to the head and face — specifically, he was missing his eyebrows. V. A. Vozrozhdenny, the forensic expert performing the post-mortem examination, judged that the soft tissue injuries to the faces and heads of those found in the stream happened post-mortem because of the location of the bodies in a running stream of water.

## Important Note on Injuries

Three of the hikers had fatal injuries: Thibeaux-Brignolles had major skull damage, and Dubinina and Zolotaryov had major chest fractures. According to Boris Vozrozhdenny, the force required to cause such damage would have been extremely high, comparable to that of a car crash. Notably, the bodies had no external wounds associated with the bone fractures, as if they had been subjected to a high level of pressure. V. A. Vozrozhdenny, the forensic expert performing the post-mortem examination, judged that the soft tissue injuries happened post-mortem because of the location of the bodies in a stream.
