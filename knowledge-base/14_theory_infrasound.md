# Theory: Infrasound

## What It Proposes

Another hypothesis popularised by Donnie Eichar's 2013 book Dead Mountain is that wind going around Kholat Syakal created a Kármán vortex street, which can produce infrasound capable of inducing panic attacks in humans.

According to Eichar's theory, the infrasound generated by the wind as it passed over the top of the Holatchahl mountain was responsible for causing physical discomfort and mental distress in the hikers. Eichar claims that, because of their panic, the hikers were driven to leave the tent by whatever means necessary and fled down the slope. By the time they were farther down the hill, they would have been out of the infrasound's path and would have regained their composure, but in the darkness would have been unable to return to their shelter. The traumatic injuries suffered by three of the victims were the result of their stumbling over the edge of a ravine in the darkness and landing on rocks at the bottom.

## Evidence Supporting It

- The shape of Kholat Syakhl is consistent with the kind of topography that can generate a Kármán vortex street when wind passes over it.
- Infrasound is a documented phenomenon capable of inducing panic, anxiety, and physical discomfort in humans, which could explain why the experienced hikers fled without gathering proper clothing or equipment.
- The theory accounts for the group regaining composure once they reached the tree line, which is consistent with the observation that the footprints show a walking pace rather than a dead run.
- The traumatic injuries to the three hikers found in the ravine are explained by a fall in darkness onto rocks at the ravine bottom, which is consistent with the absence of external wounds on the bones.

## Evidence Against It

- No specific counterarguments to the infrasound hypothesis are presented in the Wikipedia source beyond the general body of evidence that points toward weather and avalanche-related explanations.
- Donnie Eichar also evaluated several other theories and deemed them unlikely or discredited, including animal attacks, a single member being blown away by wind, and a romantic argument — but the infrasound theory itself is not explicitly refuted in the source material.

## Expert Opinions

The hypothesis was popularised by Donnie Eichar in his 2013 book Dead Mountain. Eichar also investigated and made a documentary about the incident. The theory involves the physical phenomenon of a Kármán vortex street, which is a recognised concept in fluid dynamics.

## Status

The infrasound hypothesis remains one of the notable alternative explanations for the incident. It is not among the three explanations considered when Russian authorities reopened the investigation in 2019 (avalanche, slab avalanche, hurricane). It has not been officially endorsed or officially refuted by Russian investigators.
