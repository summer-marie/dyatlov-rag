# Theory: Military Testing / Cover-up

## What It Proposes

In one speculation, the campsite fell within the path of a Soviet parachute mine exercise. This theory alleges that the hikers, woken by loud explosions, fled the tent in a shoeless panic and found themselves unable to return for supply retrieval. After some members froze to death attempting to endure the bombardment, others commandeered their clothing only to be fatally injured by subsequent parachute mine concussions.

Parachute mines detonate while still in the air rather than upon striking the Earth's surface and produce signature injuries similar to those experienced by the hikers: heavy internal damage with relatively little external trauma. The theory coincides with reported sightings of glowing, orange orbs floating or falling in the sky within the general vicinity of the hikers and allegedly photographed by them, potentially military aircraft or descending parachute mines.

A similar theory alleges the testing of radiological weapons and is based partly on the discovery of radioactivity on some of the clothing as well as the descriptions of the bodies by relatives as having orange skin and grey hair.

Researcher Andrei Shepelev hypothesized the hikers fled for shelter after mistaking a photoflash bomb from a US RB-47 reconnaissance aircraft for a nuclear explosion.

## Evidence Supporting It

- There are indeed records of parachute mines being tested by the Soviet military in the area about the time the hikers were there.
- Parachute mines detonate in the air and produce heavy internal damage with relatively little external trauma — injuries consistent with those found on Thibeaux-Brignolles, Dubinina, and Zolotaryov.
- Some levels of radiation were found on one victim's clothing, which the radiological weapons variant of this theory uses as supporting evidence.
- Another group of hikers approximately 50 kilometres south of the incident reported seeing strange orange spheres in the sky to the north on the night of the incident. Similar spheres were observed in Ivdel and adjacent areas continually during the period from February to March 1959 by various independent witnesses, including the meteorology service and the military.
- The initial suppression by Soviet authorities of files describing the group's disappearance is sometimes mentioned as evidence of a cover-up.
- Some speculate that the bodies were unnaturally manipulated, on the basis of characteristic livor mortis markings discovered during an autopsy, as well as burns to hair and skin.
- Photographs of the tent allegedly show it was erected incorrectly, something the experienced hikers were unlikely to have done.
- This theory (among others) uses scavenging animals to explain Dubinina's missing tongue and facial tissue.

## Evidence Against It

- Radioactive dispersal would have affected all, not only some, of the hikers and equipment.
- The skin and hair discolouration described by relatives can be explained by a natural process of mummification after three months of exposure to the cold and wind.
- The concealment of information about domestic incidents was standard procedure in the USSR, and thus far from peculiar. By the late 1980s, all Dyatlov files had been released in some manner.
- The possibility of a murder had been discounted when Russian authorities reopened the investigation in 2019.

## Expert Opinions

Researcher Andrei Shepelev hypothesized the hikers fled for shelter after mistaking a photoflash bomb from a US RB-47 reconnaissance aircraft for a nuclear explosion. Lev Nikitich Ivanov, who led the official inquest in 1959, published an article in 1990 that included his admission that the investigation team had no rational explanation for the incident. He also stated that, after his team reported that they had seen flying spheres, he then received direct orders from high-ranking regional officials to dismiss this claim.

## Status

The military testing theories have not been confirmed. When Russian authorities reopened the investigation in February 2019, only three possible explanations were being considered: an avalanche, a slab avalanche, or a hurricane. The possibility of a murder had been discounted. By the late 1980s, all Dyatlov files had been released in some manner.
