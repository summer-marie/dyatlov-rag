# Other Theories

## Yeti / Alma

Reviewing a sensationalist "Yeti" hypothesis, US skeptic author Benjamin Radford suggested an avalanche as more plausible. The Yeti theory was among those discussed in public following the 1990 publication of Anatoly Gushchin's book The Price of State Secrets Is Nine Lives, which concentrated on the speculative theory of a Soviet secret weapon experiment but whose publication led to public discussion stimulated by interest in the paranormal. Many of those who had remained silent for thirty years reported new facts about the accident following the book's release. The Yeti hypothesis is treated in the Wikipedia source as a sensationalist fringe theory without credible evidential support.

## UFO

Numerous theories have been put forward to account for the unexplained deaths, including the appearance of a UFO or ball lightning. Another group of hikers approximately 50 kilometres south of the incident reported that they saw strange orange spheres in the sky to the north on the night of the incident. Similar spheres were observed in Ivdel and adjacent areas continually during the period from February to March 1959 by various independent witnesses, including the meteorology service and the military. These sightings were not noted in the 1959 investigation, and the various witnesses came forward years later. Lev Nikitich Ivanov, who led the official inquest in 1959, published an article in 1990 in which he admitted that the investigation team had no rational explanation for the incident. He also stated that, after his team reported that they had seen flying spheres, he then received direct orders from high-ranking regional officials to dismiss this claim. The military testing theory proposes that the orange orbs were potentially military aircraft or descending parachute mines rather than UFOs.

## Crime of Passion

Donnie Eichar evaluated a theory proposing that an argument — possibly related to a romantic encounter that left some of them only partially clothed — led to a violent dispute. Eichar states that this is "highly implausible. By all indications, the group was largely harmonious, and sexual tension was confined to platonic flirtation and crushes. There were no drugs present and the only alcohol was a small flask of medicinal alcohol, found intact at the scene. The group had even sworn off cigarettes for the expedition." Furthermore, a fight could not have left the massive injuries that one body had suffered.

## Status of These Theories

The Yeti, UFO, and crime of passion theories are all treated as fringe or discredited explanations in the available source material. When Russian authorities reopened the investigation in February 2019, only three possible explanations were being considered: an avalanche, a slab avalanche, or a hurricane. The possibility of a murder had been discounted. The orange spheres reported by multiple independent witnesses remain unexplained, though the military testing theory offers a non-paranormal interpretation.
